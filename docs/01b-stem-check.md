# DewDrive STEM check

Verdict: the working principle (a desiccant bed that adsorbs from night air and is regenerated by the sun in a sealed box with a shaded condenser) sits within known physics and chemistry, but the design as drawn had five details that did not do what the calculation note assumed, and fixing them leaves the yield below its target. The worst was optical: the sun fell on white sorbent beads, not on the black surface DWD-CAL-001 had taken, and the design would have made 0.01 L per day (S5). The independent review then found the second largest: the tray floor passed about 25 % of the bed area to the vapour, not the 60 % taken (S9). The others were bare aluminium and stainless steel in hot CaCl₂ brine (S20), bare aluminium fins treated as a high-emissivity radiator (S12) and ground anchors with a 6 % margin (S18). All five are fixed under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23 (DWD-DEC-001, D16 to D20; DWD-DDR-005). With the fixes the design collects **0.44 L per day at the design point, below the 0.5 L target of PR-001** (DWD-CAL-001 v0.8, C3; 0.37 to 0.41 L under the sensitivities), and the ways to recover it are for Amish (DWD-DEC-001, open item 2). Of the 27 claims on option A, **18 are within known science and 9 are plausible**: S16 is within since Amish lowered the fan cut-out to 65 % RH on 2026-10-25 (DWD-DEC-001 item 3); S22 and S26 are closed by calculation, and S8, S11, S18, S21, S23, S24 and S27 need a test, a log or a supplier's rating (TRL 4). `stem_check` is therefore **open**. Options B and C sit within known science, but option B makes no water at the design point and option C makes 0.2 to 0.5 L per day (rows B1 to C3). Method: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which reads the tagged results of `docs/04-calcs/sizing.py` (DWD-CAL-001 v0.8) and checks them against independent first-order estimates.

## Fields not relevant

None. Every field is touched: physics (psychrometrics, fluid flow through the bed, radiation and optics, heat transfer, diffusion, statics), chemistry (salt-solution equilibria, thermochemistry, corrosion, polymer ratings, analytical limits), biology (microbial quality of the water, burn thresholds), mathematics (energy closure and the lumped-bed approximation) and engineering (power budget, control rule).

## Concept options

- Option A, solar desiccant box: rows S1 to S27. Within known science after the fixes; ten rows plausible; collects 0.44 L per day, below PR-001.
- Option B, radiative dew condenser: within known science (B1, B2). The panel runs about 10 K below the air, but at 20 °C and 40 % RH the dew point is 6.0 °C, so it makes no water at the design point. It is not dropped on STEM grounds; its yield is scored in DWD-OPT-001.
- Option C, liquid-desiccant glazed still: within known science (C1 to C3), with a yield of 0.2 to 0.5 L per day that depends strongly on how hot the cloth gets. The published 2.5 L per m² per day (Kabeel, 2007) does not carry over to the design point.
